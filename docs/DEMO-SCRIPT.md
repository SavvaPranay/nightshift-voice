# Demo script

Judges score "quality of the live demonstration" as its own criterion. Rehearse it 3 times.
Record a backup video at 5:30 in case the live call fails.

## Setup on stage

Laptop on the projector showing the console. Phone on speaker. Console already logged in.
One call already in the list so the screen isn't empty.

## The opening line (say this before touching anything)

> "Voice agents can talk. They can't be trusted to act. That's why businesses still pay
> humans to answer phones at night. ElevenLabs has a transfer-to-human tool, but transfer
> assumes someone's there to take the call, and after-hours is defined by nobody being there.
> So this agent finishes the call, and a human approves in the morning."

## The call, 90 seconds

1. **Dial the number on speaker.** Agent picks up, greets as the business.
2. **Ask something it knows.** "What time do you open Monday?" It answers from the knowledge
   base. *Point at the transcript filling in on screen.*
3. **Ask for a booking.** "Can I get in Tuesday at 10?" It checks availability, then says a
   person will confirm shortly. **It does not claim it's booked.**
   *Point at the pending action appearing in the console, live.*
4. **Ask for something out of policy.** "Any chance of a discount?" It declines and escalates
   rather than guessing. *Point at the escalation.*
5. **Hang up.**

## The turn, 20 seconds

> "The caller's done. Nobody was awake. Now the owner opens this in the morning."

**Click Approve.** The pending action commits. **The phone gets the confirmation.**

That beat, approve on screen and the phone buzzes, is the demo. Let it land. Don't talk over it.

## Close

> "Everything the agent did on its own was read-only or a message. Everything that costs
> money or changes a commitment waited for a person. That's the difference between a demo
> and something you'd actually put on your business line."

## If the live call fails

Switch to the recorded video without apologizing or explaining. Keep the same narration.
Say "here's a run from earlier" and carry on.

## Answering "how did you use Mel"

Be specific and honest, including where it fought you. That reads as credible to the people
who built it. Mention the investigate/repair/verify loop and the built-in code review.
